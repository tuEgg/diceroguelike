# Content design principles

Agreed with Jared in the design review of 2026-11-23 (Q17). Every new die, keepsake, core, potion or tool is checked against these. Evidence: `content-catalogue.md`, `analysis-*.md` in this folder.

## The run's shape

- **Chapter 1: get stronger.** Rewards are good on their own; you don't need a plan yet.
- **End of chapter 2: the engine hums.** By the end of chapter 2 a build's engine should be running most turns.
- **Chapter 3: enjoy it.** The payoff chapter, where the build shows off.
- So: pieces that are good alone are common and early; pieces that pull a build together become available by chapter 2.

## Archetypes

An archetype is a fantasy with an engine, not a single axis. Each is described by:
- **Fantasy:** what it feels like in chapter 3.
- **Engine:** the resource it grows.
- **Enablers and payoffs:** what gets it going, and what rewards you once it does.
- **Cost:** what it gives up.

Every new piece should feed at least one engine, as an enabler or a payoff (bridges between two are welcome).

The seven (2026-11-23):
1. **Full ink (control):** grow max ink, make ink cheaper, rewrite and move every intent.
2. **Infinite might (thin and punch):** a tiny bag, Might building every turn.
3. **Valleys (low rolls pay):** rolling a 1 is good.
4. **Coin swarm:** lots of coins as free plays, payoffs per die played.
5. **Heavy hitters:** a few huge dice, the Hammer, late slots.
6. **Fortress (block and punish):** Block that turns into damage.
7. **Alignment extremes:** commit to very low or very high alignment.

## Rules of thumb

- **Free value is never common.** Anything that gives value without using a play (coins, Stowaways, "+1 play", per-play keepsakes) is worth about a whole extra die, because plays are the limit: +1 play is worth about twice +1 die dealt (sim with ink, and Jared's playtests). It's uncommon or rarer, and kept small.
- **Ink is a weak currency today:** a point is worth about 0.2-0.5 of an attack pip, so a 3-ink reroll is worth ~1 pip. Full ink needs ink to buy more (or cost less) before it can be an archetype.
- **Every core shape needs a payoff.** A core's shape must matter to something in the game: "rolled its middle" for Bell and Dome, "second-highest" for Dual, min/max for Edge and Binary, totals for Loaded and Weighted. A shape nothing rewards is dead content.

- **Rare is like Slay the Spire's gold cards:** clearly above the power curve on its own, and usually build-defining or rule-bending too. A rare below an uncommon (Tidepusher today) is a bug.

## Keywords

- **Overboard:** after its slot resolves, the die is gone for the rest of the fight (it's back in the bag after). Taking it back before End Turn is still free. All heal dice have it, unchanged otherwise: healing is meant to be scarce. Some other strong dice have it too.

## Rule changes agreed (2026-11-23, to build)

- **Hammer:** a merge loses 2 sides (d4+d4 = d6, d6+d4 = d8, d6+d6 = d10). Sim: 2/3/4 merges go from +16/+33/+44% to +6/+18/+27%.
- **Drill:** works as many times as you like in a workbench visit (cores share the 3 item slots, 5 with the Toolbelt, so that's the limit).
- **Builders count as played:** dice that build a slot fire "when played" effects, keepsakes and their own.
- **Per chapter:** the elite, bounty and crossroads counters reset each chapter.
- **Treasure:** a fixed treasure stop opens spread 3 of every chapter, before the two drafted pages; treasures are no longer forced onto pages.
- **Overboard:** no safety net. Running out of dice is the cost of a thin Overboard build.
- **Item rewards (B162, built):** +20% per fight without one, -30% after one, never below 10%.
- **Rarity ladder for archetypes:** each archetype gets common enablers (dice in fight rewards), uncommon payoffs (dice and shop items) and a rare or boss engine piece that makes it sing. Any run can start one from commons.
- **Ink:** base ink costs stay; the Full ink archetype gets its own pieces (raise max ink, cut costs, pay off spending).
